## Step 3: Reconciliation

### D01. Anomaly treatment set to `excluded` (Abhinav's decision, confirmed by the data)

- **Date:** 26 September 2026
- **Decision:** The 15 negative amounts on invoices marked paid (P06) contribute zero to the cube. `cfg_settings.anomaly_treatment = 'excluded'`, set in `sql/02_clean.sql`.
- **Alternatives:** `as_reported` (keep the negatives) and `flipped` (reverse the sign).
- **Evidence:** `outputs/tables/step3_anomaly_options.csv`. Under `excluded` the cube ties to the management accounts' product lines in 48 of 48 months with no residual; under the other two options it ties in 36. In all 15 cases the prior and next month bill the same amount as a positive, so the rows look like sign-entry errors, and reported revenue is probably understated by their absolute value (£13,843.82 over four years; by year in `outputs/tables/step3_reported_total_effects.csv`). Immaterial. Raised as Q11.
- **Reverse:** change the value in `cfg_settings` in `sql/02_clean.sql` and rerun. `revenue_cube(t)` and `mrr_cube(t)` and their tests cover all three options.

### D02. In the four round-number months, the sum of the product lines is taken as correct (Abhinav's decision, confirmed by the data)

- **Date:** 26 September 2026
- **Decision:** For June 2023, March 2024, November 2024 and February 2025, the invoice-based figure (which equals the sum of the management product lines) is treated as the supportable revenue. The difference to the reported total is labelled `unexplained`: a manual adjustment that was never allocated to a product line.
- **Alternatives:** trust the reported total and treat the product lines as wrong; or split the difference.
- **Evidence:** `outputs/tables/step3_unexplained_items.csv`. The cube ties to the lines in each month to the penny. The reported total differs by round amounts (+£18,500, −£12,000, +£9,500, −£15,000, cube less reported). Effect on the reported total by year: 2023 £18,500 below the invoices, 2024 £2,500 above, 2025 £15,000 above (`step3_reported_total_effects.csv`). June 2023 is 0.681% of the month's reported revenue, above the 0.5% monthly threshold; no year breaches the 0.1% annual threshold. Raised as Q07, Q12, Q13 and Q14.
- **Reverse:** none needed in the data; the cube is unaffected. If management shows the adjustments are valid, relabel the months in `sql/04_reconciliation.sql`.

### D04. Materiality thresholds and status labels

- **Date:** 26 September 2026
- **Decision:** Flag a month if |difference| > £1,000. A flagged month is material above 0.5% of its reported revenue. A year is material above 0.1% of annual revenue. Labels: `tied` (no difference, nothing to explain), `explained` (no difference once the anomaly invoices are excluded, as management did), `unexplained` (the reported total differs from the invoices).
- **Alternatives:** a single percentage threshold; labelling the anomaly months `tied` because they show no difference under the chosen setting.
- **Evidence:** £1,000 is 0.03% to 0.05% of a month's revenue, enough to catch one mid-sized invoice. Labelling anomaly months `explained` keeps the record of why they tie.
- **Reverse:** edit `cfg_materiality` and the CASE expression in `sql/04_reconciliation.sql`.

### D31. Office files and the PDF are normalised after they are saved

### D33. Explicit ordering everywhere a row order or a sum order can reach an output

- **Date:** 26 September 2026
- **Decision:** `run_all.py` gives every child process, including the notebook kernels, `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`. The 04e revenue bridge sums customers in `sorted()` order instead of set order. The five analysis queries that lacked one got an `ORDER BY` on their grain, and so did the five `COPY` exports. Multi-row `UNION ALL` tables and notebook queries now carry an ordinal column that is sorted on and then dropped, so rows keep their written order. `cleaning_actions` and `clean_removed_invoices` are ordered by action and record. `preserve_insertion_order` is set explicitly on every connection; it is DuckDB's default, and it is what makes a plain scan of a table created with `ORDER BY` return rows in that order.
- **Evidence:** the 04e revenue bridge printed `-0.0` or `0.0` for a check value depending on the run, because string hashing changes set order and so the order of a floating-point sum. I could not confirm DuckDB's documented order guarantees for `UNION ALL`, because duckdb.org is blocked from this session, so the order is made explicit instead of assumed.
- **Reverse:** remove the `ORDER BY` clauses and ordinals; outputs may then depend on DuckDB's execution order.
